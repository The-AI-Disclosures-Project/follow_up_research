# Survival Analysis of ChatGPT Conversation Duration

**Research Question**: Do follow-up suggestions extend conversation length, or is the observed increase in conversation duration driven by model capability improvements over time?

## Key Findings

### 1. The Tail Is Getting Heavier Over Time

| Year | Pareto α | Interpretation |
|------|----------|----------------|
| 2023 | 3.51 | Lighter tail |
| 2024 | 3.18 | Moderate |
| 2025 | 2.82 | **Heavier tail** |

Lower α = more probability mass in extreme-length conversations. The distribution's upper tail is expanding.

### 2. Suggestion Effect Is Counterintuitive

**Finding**: Conversations where suggestions are shown have **higher** hazard of ending (OR = 1.14, p < 0.001).

**Interpretation**: This suggests a **selection effect**—the algorithm shows suggestions at natural stopping points, not that suggestions cause earlier termination.

### 3. Year Effects After Controlling for Suggestions

| Comparison | Odds Ratio | Meaning |
|------------|------------|---------|
| 2024 vs 2023 | 1.41 | 41% higher hazard of ending |
| 2025 vs 2023 | 1.27 | 27% higher hazard of ending |

Newer years show higher hazard despite more advanced models, suggesting **model capability effects dominate**: better models resolve queries faster.

### 4. Cannot Cleanly Separate Model vs Year Effects

GPT versions are highly collinear with year:
- 2023: gpt-3.5-turbo, gpt-4-0314
- 2024: gpt-4o, gpt-4o-mini
- 2025: gpt-4o-mini, gpt-4.1-mini

Only `gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18` spans both 2024 and 2025, providing limited within-model variation.

## Methods

### Discrete-Time Hazard Model

We model conversation duration using logistic regression on the turn-level data:

```
P(end at turn t | survived to t) = logit⁻¹(β₀ + β₁·log(t) + β₂·suggestion + β₃·year)
```

- **Unit of analysis**: Turn-level (each turn where conversation could end)
- **Outcome**: `event = 1` if conversation ended, `0` if continued
- **Key predictors**:
  - `log_turn`: Captures declining hazard over conversation length
  - `suggestion_given`: Whether follow-up suggestions were shown
  - `year`: Controls for temporal trends

### Split-Population Model

Given that 68.7% of conversations end at turn 1, we also fit separate models for:
1. **First-turn hazard**: Who continues at all?
2. **Later-turn hazard**: Given continuation, who persists?

This captures the distinct populations of single-query vs. multi-turn users.

### Power-Law Tail Analysis

We fit a Pareto distribution to the survival function:

```
P(T ≥ t) ∝ t^(-α)    for t ≥ 5
```

The exponent α characterizes the tail heaviness.

## Data Requirements

The analysis requires `analysis_data_flattened.jsonl` with fields:
- `conversation_hash`: Unique conversation ID
- `turn`: Turn number within conversation
- `turns`: Total turns in conversation
- `conversation_continued`: Boolean outcome
- `suggestion_given_count`: Number of suggestions shown
- `year`: Year of conversation
- `model`: GPT model version
- `user_word_count`, `assistant_word_count`: Content length

## Limitations

1. **Selection on suggestions**: Without randomization, we cannot identify causal effect of suggestions
2. **Model-year collinearity**: Cannot cleanly separate model capability from temporal trends
3. **Sample selection**: WildChat users may not represent typical ChatGPT users
4. **Unobserved heterogeneity**: Some users inherently more engaged; frailty models partially address this
